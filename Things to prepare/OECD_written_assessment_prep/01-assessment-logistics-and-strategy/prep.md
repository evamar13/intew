# Assessment Logistics And Strategy

## What The Communication Tells You

- The written assessment opens from the date of invitation.
- You must complete it within `7 days`.
- Once you begin the first activity, you have `48 hours` to finish and submit.
- The timer keeps running even when you close the platform.
- The full assessment duration shown is `2880 minutes`, which signals a long window, not a short live test.
- You should use Chrome on a laptop or desktop with a stable connection.
- The activity instructions also say that once a question starts, you cannot pause or return.

## What This Means In Practice

This is not only a knowledge test. It is also an execution test. OECD is indirectly testing whether you can:

- plan before acting
- manage time and ambiguity
- read instructions carefully
- produce reliable work without rushing
- work in a disciplined, self-managed way

The key practical point is that the invitation email and the activity instructions should be read together:

- before starting, you still control when to begin
- after starting, the safe assumption is that you should not rely on closing the platform and resuming later
- even if you close it, the timer continues to run

## What You Need To Prepare Before Starting

### Technical setup

- Use one quiet workspace with stable internet.
- Open only what you are permitted to use.
- Keep a clock visible outside the platform as backup.
- Have water, charger, and notes ready before starting.

### Working setup

- Prepare one note file for rough planning.
- Prepare a simple response template:
- issue
- analysis
- options
- recommendation
- implementation
- risks
- Decide in advance how much time you will spend on planning, writing, and editing.

### Mental setup

- Expect a task that requires judgment, not perfect technical mastery.
- Expect incomplete information.
- Expect that clarity and structure matter as much as content.

## Recommended Time Strategy

If you receive one major written task:

- `10 to 15%` of time: read instructions and define deliverable
- `15 to 20%`: identify core issue, audience, and criteria
- `45 to 55%`: write first full draft
- `15 to 20%`: tighten structure, improve logic, remove repetition
- `10%`: final proofread against task requirements

If you receive multiple activities:

- estimate effort first
- do not spend too much time perfecting the first answer
- keep a reserve block at the end for review

## Questions You Must Answer Before Writing

- Who is the audience?
- What format is expected: memo, essay, briefing note, recommendation, analysis?
- Am I being asked to explain, compare, recommend, or design?
- What is the main decision problem?
- What evidence do I have?
- What limitations or uncertainties must I acknowledge?
- What would a practical OECD-style recommendation look like?

## Rule Interpretation You Should Follow

Because the written instructions are stricter than the general email wording, the safest interpretation is:

- do not start until fully ready
- do not assume you can interrupt and return safely
- do not use the internet, AI tools, books, or other people unless the task explicitly allows it
- do not include your name, email, or other identifying information in the file

## What OECD Is Likely Looking For In Your Process

- disciplined reading of instructions
- calm handling of ambiguity
- prioritisation
- quality under time limits
- professional tone
- evidence-based reasoning

## Common Failure Modes

- starting to write before defining the actual task
- giving a generic answer that could fit any organisation
- writing too much background and too little analysis
- forgetting the audience
- not making a recommendation
- ignoring implementation steps
- ignoring risk, ethics, or limitations

## Your Pre-Start Checklist

- I know when I will start.
- I have a quiet block large enough to work well.
- I have a note-taking system ready.
- I have a writing template ready.
- I understand that the timer continues after exit.
- I understand that once the question starts, I should treat the exercise as non-pausable.
- I will not open the first activity until I am ready to work seriously.
