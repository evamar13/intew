# Written Exercise Method And Essay Structure

## What The Exercise Is Probably Testing

- analysis
- prioritisation
- clarity
- judgment
- relevance to the role
- ability to communicate to a professional audience

The strongest answers will be structured, concise, evidence-based, and useful.

Based on the OECD activity library visible in Maki, this exercise is also likely testing explicit dimensions such as:

- written proficiency
- analytical thinking
- interpretation and synthesis
- logical structure
- problem decomposition
- policy evaluation

## Default Structure For Most Answers

### 1. Brief introduction

- define the issue
- state why it matters to OECD
- signal the structure of your response

### 2. Analysis

- explain the problem
- identify relevant drivers or constraints
- separate facts from assumptions

### 3. Options or possible approaches

- compare realistic approaches
- show trade-offs
- avoid presenting false binaries

### 4. Recommendation

- say what OECD should do
- justify why this is the best option
- show proportionality and practicality

### 5. Implementation

- next steps
- owners or functions involved
- quick wins vs medium-term actions
- metrics or review points

### 6. Risks and limitations

- explain uncertainty
- mention operational or reputational risks
- show how you would mitigate them

## A Fast Planning Method

Before writing, create six short bullets:

- objective
- audience
- key argument
- 2 to 4 supporting points
- recommendation
- risk/caveat

If you cannot fill those six bullets, you are not ready to draft.

## How To Make The Answer Sound Strong

### Be specific

Instead of:

- "OECD should improve AI communications analytics."

Write:

- "OECD should pilot a small set of AI-era indicators alongside existing communications metrics, with clear caveats and a review cycle before full integration into reporting."

### Show judgment

Instead of:

- "Track everything available."

Write:

- "I would prioritise a limited set of indicators that are decision-relevant, methodologically defensible, and easy to interpret across directorates."

### Show institutional awareness

Instead of:

- "This will help the brand."

Write:

- "This will help the OECD assess how its evidence reaches and influences target audiences in policy, media, and AI-mediated environments."

## What To Include In Most Role-Relevant Answers

- a distinction between visibility and impact
- a caution about incomplete measurement
- a recommendation that is operational, not theoretical
- an acknowledgment of ethics, inclusion, or reputational considerations
- a cross-team element where relevant
- a structure that looks like a professional workplace note rather than an academic essay

## What The Platform-Style Skill Labels Likely Mean For Writing

### Written Proficiency

- the answer should read like a useful internal note
- the prose should be clear, economical, and professional

### Analytical Thinking

- identify the real problem
- break it into parts
- connect evidence to conclusion

### Policy Evaluation

- compare options
- explain trade-offs
- recommend one path and justify it

### Adaptability

- show comfort with AI-era uncertainty and change
- avoid rigid thinking

### Achieve Results

- make the answer actionable
- include implementation logic and priorities

## What To Cut During Editing

- repeated background
- tool name dumping
- unsupported claims
- vague enthusiasm about AI
- long definitions that do not advance the answer

## Final Editing Checklist

- Did I answer the actual question?
- Is my recommendation explicit?
- Is my structure easy to scan?
- Did I distinguish evidence from inference?
- Did I mention limitations where needed?
- Does this sound useful to an OECD manager?
- Did I keep the tone professional and calm?
- Does this show written proficiency, analytical thinking, and policy usefulness rather than just knowledge?
