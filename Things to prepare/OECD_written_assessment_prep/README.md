## What You Need To Review

### 1. Assessment logistics and execution

- The assessment is available for `7 days` from invitation.
- Once started, you have `48 hours` to complete it.
- The platform timer keeps running even if you close the platform.
- The safer interpretation of the instructions is that once the question starts, you should assume you cannot safely pause and return.
- You should complete it on a laptop or desktop, in Chrome, with a stable connection.
- You need a practical time-management plan before you click `Get started`.

### 2. OECD institutional context

- OECD mission, public-policy role, and member-country orientation.
- The role of the Directorate for Communications.
- The role of the Communications Impact, Standards and Capacity Building Division.
- Why the OECD cares about reach, influence, policy uptake, trust, and reputation.

### 3. The actual job mandate

- AI-mediated visibility: LLM referrals, generative search, bot traffic, AI discoverability.
- Communications intelligence: media shifts, monitoring, interpretation, targeted briefs.
- Publication and policy impact: downloads, citations, academic and policy footprint.
- Performance reporting: dashboards, reporting tools, indicator design, data quality.
- Working style: analytical, structured, detail-oriented, collaborative, experimentation-minded.

### 4. Core technical concepts to revise

- Difference between visibility, reach, influence, and impact.
- Difference between human traffic, crawler traffic, referral traffic, and AI bot traffic.
- What GEO and AI discoverability mean in practice.
- Limits of metrics and proxies when direct measurement is incomplete.
- How to design indicators that are useful, cautious, and decision-oriented.
- Basic dashboard logic: inputs, outputs, trends, segments, caveats, decisions.

### 5. Writing skills likely to be assessed

- Turning messy information into a short structured argument.
- Explaining technical issues in plain language for non-technical stakeholders.
- Prioritising actions instead of listing observations.
- Showing judgment under uncertainty.
- Balancing innovation with risk, ethics, and evidence.
- Writing clearly, briefly, and in a way that is easy for decision-makers to use.

### 7. OECD competencies you should demonstrate

For a junior written assessment, you should mainly show strong `Level 1 - Execute` behavior, with occasional signs of `Level 2 - Inspire` maturity.

- Vision and strategy
- Strengthening people

- Collaboration and horizontality

- Innovation and acceptance of change

## Likely Written Exercise Themes

You should be ready for one or more of these:

- A memo on how OECD can monitor visibility in AI systems.
- A briefing note on a communications trend linked to AI.
- A short methodology proposal for measuring publication impact.
- A recommendation note on metrics, dashboards, or reporting improvements.
- A scenario response involving reputational risk, data ambiguity, or stakeholder pressure.
- A synthesis exercise where you compare possible approaches and recommend one.

## What A Strong Answer Should Show

- It understands the OECD's mission and public-service context.
- It is structured around a problem, evidence, options, recommendation, and implementation.
- It uses metrics carefully and distinguishes signal from noise.
- It acknowledges limitations instead of overstating certainty.
- It is practical and decision-oriented.
- It reflects ethics, inclusiveness, and cross-team collaboration.
- It sounds like someone who can execute reliably in a complex institution.

## What To Avoid

- Sounding like a generic AI enthusiast without institutional judgment.
- Treating AI metrics as exact when they are often indirect or partial.
- Confusing activity metrics with real communications impact.
- Ignoring stakeholders, governance, or reputational risk.
- Writing in a long academic style without a clear recommendation.
- Focusing only on tools instead of purpose, quality, and use by decision-makers.
- Using inflated language without operational detail.
