# Competency: Collaboration And Horizontality

## What It Means At OECD

This competency is about working across teams, reducing silos, sharing information early, building strategic relationships, and communicating with tact, diplomacy, respect, and intercultural sensitivity.

For this role, this is highly relevant because communications intelligence depends on coordination across:

- communications teams
- analysts
- policy directorates
- digital teams
- leadership stakeholders

## What You Need To Show In Writing

- you understand that analytics should serve many teams
- you value early knowledge sharing
- you can communicate recommendations in a tactful, practical way
- you understand cross-functional dependency

## How To Demonstrate It

Write recommendations that:

- involve the right internal partners
- encourage standardised but shared approaches
- avoid building isolated reporting systems
- recognise different stakeholder needs
- balance clarity with diplomacy

## Examples Of Strong Signals

- "A cross-directorate reporting approach would improve consistency while preserving flexibility for specialised audiences."
- "The methodology should be developed with communications, analytics, and digital stakeholders to ensure usability and comparability."
- "Early sharing of emerging signals would help priority teams respond before issues become larger."

## What Assessors May Notice

Even in a written essay, they may infer whether you are someone who:

- collaborates
- respects different functions
- communicates constructively
- sees the institution as a whole

## What Weak Answers Look Like

- acting as if one analyst can solve everything alone
- ignoring internal coordination
- using rigid, one-size-fits-all recommendations
- sounding combative or dismissive toward other teams

## Self-Check

- Did I identify who needs to be involved?
- Did I show how information should be shared?
- Did I sound tactful and realistic?
- Did I avoid siloed thinking?
