# OECD Context And Role Mandate

## OECD Context You Need To Understand

The OECD is not a commercial brand. It is an international policy organisation serving member countries. That changes how communications intelligence should be understood.

Key implications:

- communications are tied to public-policy relevance, not just visibility
- credibility, neutrality, trust, and evidence matter heavily
- influence can happen through media, policy, academia, and institutional uptake
- stakeholder needs are diverse and international
- communications analysis must support strategic decisions, not vanity metrics

## Directorate Context

The Directorate for Communications exists to maximise the reach and impact of OECD work through clear, evidence-based communications across the organisation.

This means the role sits at the intersection of:

- strategic communications
- measurement
- institutional reputation
- digital platforms
- cross-directorate coordination

## Division Context

The Communications Impact, Standards and Capacity Building Division focuses on:

- strategic analysis
- performance evaluation
- reputation monitoring
- response capability against mis- and disinformation
- building communications capability
- setting and maintaining standards
- guidance on reputational risk and rights management

## What The Role Is Really For

The role is not just "AI person in communications." It is an analyst role for measuring how OECD content is found, used, and trusted in changing information environments.

You need to show that you understand four layers of value:

### 1. Measurement

- what visibility looks like
- what can be measured directly
- what requires proxies
- how to interpret trends carefully

### 2. Strategic interpretation

- why a pattern matters
- what risk or opportunity it creates
- what decision should follow

### 3. Communication relevance

- which outputs matter most
- which audiences matter most
- how findings support communication priorities

### 4. Institutional reliability

- data quality
- methodological caution
- reproducibility
- governance and credibility

## What The Ideal Candidate Profile Signals

The job description repeatedly points to these traits:

- analytical
- structured
- curious about AI
- able to synthesize
- able to explain complex material clearly
- comfortable with data and interpretation
- careful and detail-oriented
- collaborative

Your written assessment should sound like someone with those traits.

## How To Sound Aligned With The Role

Use language that reflects:

- evidence and interpretation, not hype
- experimentation with guardrails
- operational usefulness
- cross-team collaboration
- accountability for data quality
- openness to emerging methods without overstating certainty

## What Weak Alignment Looks Like

- talking only about AI tools
- ignoring communications strategy
- ignoring policy and academic uptake
- treating dashboards as the goal instead of a decision tool
- sounding overly technical for a communications role
- sounding too generic for an OECD public-policy environment
