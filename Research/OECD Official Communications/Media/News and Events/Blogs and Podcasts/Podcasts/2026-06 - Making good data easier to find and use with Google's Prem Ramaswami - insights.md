# Making good data easier to find and use with Google's Prem Ramaswami

## Content Type

- `Podcast`
- Series: `Statistics in the AI Era`

## Participants

- Host: `Steve MacFeely`
- Guest: `Prem Ramaswami`

## Core Insights

### 1. The central issue is no longer only producing good statistics

- In an AI-mediated information environment, statistical organisations must not only produce high-quality statistics.
- They must also remain:
  - visible
  - findable
  - trusted
  - understandable
  - correctly interpreted by both humans and machines

### 2. Google frames the problem as a data usability problem

- Prem Ramaswami describes Data Commons as an open-source Google platform designed to make the world's public statistical data universally accessible and useful.
- His core argument is that many policy and social-impact problems are constrained not by lack of data, but by the difficulty of combining and using data across separate databases, institutions and formats.

### 3. Fragmented data creates a heavy technical tax

- Public-interest decisions often require combining multiple datasets collected by different organisations in different formats.
- Today, much expert time is spent on basic harmonisation work rather than analysis.
- This means many decision-makers are effectively flying blind because data remains too hard to use.

### 4. AI increases the value of authoritative, sourceable data

- If AI systems are going to support decision-making, their outputs need to be grounded in useful, authoritative data that can be sourced back.
- This makes official statistics strategically important in the AI era, but only if they are usable by AI systems.

### 5. AI promises broader access, but also raises misinformation risk

- Ramaswami compares the current AI shift to earlier internet-era changes in access to expert knowledge.
- The benefit is wider access to information.
- The risk is that mis- and disinformation can spread more easily if systems surface poor-quality or decontextualised content.

### 6. Statistics should not remain a closed expert domain

- One of the interview's strongest ideas is that statistics cannot remain a gilded field accessible mainly to specialists.
- AI can help broaden data use among non-specialists if done responsibly.
- That matters because more people using data in their decision-making is treated as a public good.

### 7. Quality in the AI era depends on sourcing, auditability and reproducibility

Ramaswami's quality frame for data emphasises that information should be:

- well sourced
- auditable
- reproducible

This is especially important when AI systems repackage or restate statistical information.

### 8. Source attribution is one of the main practical trust mechanisms

- Google has been working to show where information comes from, including in search results.
- The idea is that users trust information more when they can see the source behind it.
- This is also one of the main ways to reduce the effects of hallucinations, even if it does not eliminate them fully.

### 9. Hallucinations are still a real limitation

- The interview is explicit that hallucinations still exist in AI systems.
- A major challenge is therefore how to use official statistical data to ground future systems so they produce more trustworthy, data-aware outputs.

### 10. The path forward is from language-only models to tool-using systems

- Ramaswami distinguishes between early large language models that mainly predict the next word and newer systems that can call tools.
- These systems can increasingly move from text generation to:
  - issuing instructions
  - calling databases
  - querying repositories of knowledge
  - retrieving source-backed information

This is a key technical insight for official statistics: AI systems become more reliable when they retrieve from trusted repositories rather than only generate from model memory.

### 11. AI can prioritise authoritative information, but only if systems are built for it

- Ramaswami's answer is not a simple yes or no.
- AI systems can prioritise authoritative information such as official statistics, but it depends on how the system is designed.
- This means discoverability and trust are partly a data problem and partly a system-design problem.

### 12. Official statistics becoming invisible is a valid concern

- The host raises the fear that users will increasingly stop visiting official statistical websites directly.
- The interview treats this as a legitimate concern.
- If AI intermediaries become the main interface, official statistics offices need to ensure their data is optimised for those systems.

### 13. AI-readiness is a strategic requirement for official data

The interview strongly implies that official data needs to be:

- machine-readable
- well structured
- linkable to authoritative sources
- easy for AI systems to retrieve and cite

Without that, trusted public data may lose practical visibility even if its quality remains high.
