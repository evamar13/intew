# AIGO And ONE AI Context

## About The OECD Working Party On Artificial Intelligence Governance (AIGO)

The OECD's Digital Policy Committee (DPC) has a Working Party on Artificial Intelligence Governance (`AIGO`) to oversee its work on Artificial Intelligence policy.

OECD member governments nominate working party members who are primarily national officials responsible for AI policies in their respective countries.

## What AIGO Oversees

AIGO oversees and gives direction to the DPC work programme on AI policy and governance. This includes:

- analysis of the design, implementation, monitoring, and evaluation of national AI policies and action plans
- AI impact assessment
- approaches for trustworthy and accountable AI
- supervising measurement and data efforts as part of the OECD.AI Observatory's `trends & data` pillar
- foresight work on AI and related emerging technologies

## Additional AIGO Functions

AIGO also:

- supports the implementation of OECD standards relating to AI
- serves as a forum for exchanging experience and documenting approaches for advancing trustworthy AI that benefits people and planet
- develops tools, methods, and guidance to advance the responsible stewardship of trustworthy AI, including the OECD.AI Policy Observatory
- supports collaboration between governments and other stakeholders on assessing and managing AI risks
- conducts outreach to non-OECD member countries to support implementation of OECD standards relating to AI

## Core Priority

AIGO's key priority is to help governments implement the `OECD AI Principles`.

### Five values-based principles for trustworthy, human-centric AI

- `Benefit People & Planet`
- `Human rights, values & fairness`
- `Transparent & explainable`
- `Robust, secure & safe`
- `Accountable`

### Five recommendations for national policies, for AI ecosystems to benefit societies

- `Investing in AI R&D`
- `Data, infrastructure, software & knowledge`
- `Regulation, testbeds and documentation`
- `Skills and labour markets`
- `International co-operation`

## AI Policy Cycle Focus

AIGO leverages lessons learned and analyses from other OECD bodies, as well as the database of national AI policies at `OECD.AI`.

The working party focuses on the practical implementation of the OECD AI Principles throughout the AI policy cycle for:

- `Policy design` - review national AI governance policies and approaches
- `Policy implementation` - identify lessons in national implementation examples
- `Policy intelligence` - identify evaluation methods and monitoring exercises
- `Policy coordination` - find approaches to international and multi-stakeholder co-operation on AI policy

The working party also provides analysis and good practices to inform policy on:

- AI sandboxes
- natural language technologies

## Prior OECD AI Policy Work Mentioned

This work builds on the OECD.AI expert group on national AI policies that operated from `February 2020` to `March 2022` and published the report `State of Implementation of the OECD AI Principles: Insights from National AI Policies` on `22 June 2021`.

That report examined how countries were implementing the five recommendations to governments contained in the OECD AI Principles and identified emerging trends in AI policy.

## OECD.AI Network Of Experts

The `OECD.AI Network of Experts` works with AIGO as an informal advisory group from:

- government
- business
- academia
- civil society

The network provides AI-specific policy advice for OECD work on AI policy and contributes to the `OECD Policy Observatory on AI`, `OECD.AI`.

## Network Function

The network provides the OECD with an `on the ground` perspective on AI and is a forum where the OECD can share information with other international initiatives and organisations.

The network:

- raises issues about trustworthy AI and other policy initiatives with the OECD
- supports international co-operation where useful
- offers a platform for in-depth discussion about shared AI policy opportunities and challenges

## Direct Link To The AI Wonk

Part of the OECD's mission to raise awareness around implementing the OECD AI Principles is to ensure that open dialogue shapes trustworthy AI.

To create a wider public dialogue, the OECD has `The AI Wonk` blog, where AI experts can present current issues.

## Network Member Profile

The network brings together AI experts from many sectors and backgrounds, including:

- AI policy experts from national governments, international organisations, other institutions, and the private sector
- AI technical experts, including researchers, computer scientists, and engineers
- experts from social sciences and humanities, including legal and ethical AI specialists

National and stakeholder delegations either renew their representatives or nominate new ones every year.

## Who Nominates Delegates

Official delegates of AIGO and members of the OECD.AI Network of Experts are nominated by OECD members and DPC partner countries.

Also participating are:

- the `European Commission`
- `Business at OECD (BIAC)`
- `Trade Union Advisory Committee (TUAC)`
- `Civil Society Information Society Advisory Council (CSISAC)`
- `Internet Technical Advisory Committee (ITAC)`

The network also includes representatives from international initiatives and organisations such as:

- `Council of Europe`
- `Inter-American Development Bank`
- `United Nations`
- `UNESCO`
- `World Bank`

The OECD Secretariat also identifies additional experts with complementary expertise to ensure a balanced multidisciplinary network, taking into account stakeholder, gender, and regional representation.

## Historical Note

The OECD.AI Network of Experts builds on the OECD's first group of AI experts, which developed a proposal that formed the basis for the `OECD AI Principles` adopted in `May 2019`.
