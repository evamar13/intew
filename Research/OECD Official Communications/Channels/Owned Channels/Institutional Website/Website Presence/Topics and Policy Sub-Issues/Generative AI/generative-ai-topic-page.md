## Page Description

Generative AI (GenAI) is a category of AI that can create new content such as text, images, videos and music. It gained global attention in `2022` with text-to-image generators and large language models (LLMs).

While GenAI has the potential to revolutionise entire industries and society, it also poses critical challenges and considerations that policymakers must confront.

## Context

### AI use by individuals is surging across the OECD although uptake is uneven

More than one-third of individuals across the OECD used generative AI tools in `2025`, highlighting how rapidly AI is becoming part of everyday life. Uptake remains uneven across population groups.

The largest divide is by age, with a gap of `53.6` percentage points, while differences by educational attainment and income level are also significant, both at around `21` percentage points. By contrast, the gender gap in AI use is relatively small, at `4.2` percentage points.

Usage is particularly high among students:

- three-quarters of students aged 16 and over reported use of generative AI tools

Adoption is also widespread among those connected to the labour market:

- people in employment: `41.1%`
- unemployed people: `36.7%`

By contrast, retired and other inactive groups report much lower usage:

- retired and other inactive groups: `12.5%`

### The G7 predicts GenAI's most important opportunities

All G7 members view productivity gains and promoting innovation and entrepreneurship as among the major opportunities made possible by generative AI. Improving healthcare follows closely, as does helping to solve the climate crisis.

### GenAI's biggest risks, according to the G7

G7 members see disinformation and the associated manipulation of opinions as the biggest risk posed by generative AI. Most G7 members also consider intellectual property right infringement and threats to privacy as major risks.
