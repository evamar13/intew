# Tomo: Maki's Interview Co-pilot

## What problem Tomo is meant to solve

Maki positions the interview as hiring’s last major blind spot: high-stakes, highly subjective, and often poorly documented.

The core argument is that:

- ATS systems store applications but do not judge candidates
- earlier stages of the funnel may be instrumented
- the human interview often remains manual, inconsistent, and hard to defend afterward

Tomo is presented as the system designed to close that gap.

## Tomo’s role in the hiring stack

Tomo is described as Maki’s `Interview Co-pilot`, designed to bring structure, completeness, and defensible evidence into human-led interviews without adding extra administrative burden to interviewers.

## Maki’s broader end-to-end hiring model

The Tomo article places the product inside a broader six-part funnel:

- Intake
- CV screening through Skills Screening
- phone screening through Voice Screening
- in-depth assessment through Deep Assessment
- scheduling and orchestration
- the human-led interview

The interview is described as the least-instrumented but most decisive step.

## Why interviews are treated as the weakest step

The article highlights several recurring problems with traditional interviews:

- outcomes depend on who asks the questions
- interviewers may remember different moments
- good answers can be forgotten during long interviews
- notes are often incomplete or scattered
- many interviews are run by managers who do not interview often
- final decisions may be hard to reconstruct if challenged later

## What Tomo does

### Before the interview

Tomo starts from a structured interview plan built around role and competency requirements.

This is intended to ensure:

- candidates for the same role are assessed on the same themes
- the interview aligns with the actual success criteria of the role

### During the interview

Tomo can join:

- Zoom
- Teams
- Meet

It then:

- captures the conversation
- transcribes live
- takes structured notes against the interview plan
- keeps the competency plan visible
- flags time pressure on competencies
- highlights promising statements for deeper follow-up

The intended outcome is that the interviewer can stay present in the conversation instead of splitting attention between listening and note-taking.

### After the interview

Tomo places the output on the candidate profile inside Maki, including:

- an AI report shaped to the team’s workflow
- direct push into the ATS
- evidence per competency
- transcript-backed rationale
- the full transcript
- plan coverage, including what was explored or skipped
- coaching-oriented insights

## What makes Tomo different from a generic AI note-taker

Maki argues that Tomo should not be understood as a generic transcriber. The distinction it emphasizes is structure.

According to the article, generic meeting recorders:

- capture unstructured conversations
- do not correct inconsistency in interview design
- do not connect observations to validated competencies

Tomo, by contrast, is described as:

- purpose-built for hiring
- connected to Maki’s assessment science
- tied to role-specific competencies
- grounded in transcript evidence
- designed to make conclusions auditable and defensible

## Defensibility and auditability

The article repeatedly frames Tomo as a defensibility layer.

Its logic is:

- each interview follows a validated structure
- evidence is tied to competencies
- observations are linked to exact transcript content
- conclusions can be reconstructed if questioned later

This is presented as especially important when:

- candidates ask how a decision was made
- works councils request explanation
- regulators require evidence

## Claimed business outcomes

The Tomo article ties the product to broader Maki performance claims, including:

- up to `90%` of screening and assessment work automated
- `3x` faster time-to-hire
- up to `25%` lower early-tenure turnover
- `98%` candidate NPS
- deployment in `60+ countries`
- approval by `80+ works councils and employee committees`

## Core positioning statement

Tomo is presented as the final layer that turns interviews from memory-based conversations into structured, defensible evidence connected to the rest of the hiring funnel.
