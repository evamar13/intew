# The Maki Manifesto: The Era of Compound Intelligence

## I. Work is the biggest system we still run blind

Work shapes economies, societies, identities, and personal well-being, yet talent decisions are still often made using outdated systems. Traditional HR systems were built for record-keeping rather than intelligence. As a result:

- processes became manual
- decisions became inconsistent
- the most valuable signal in the enterprise, human potential, remained largely invisible

Maki’s argument is that organizations still fail to measure what matters. Instead of focusing on skills, behavior, judgment, context, and outcomes, many systems still center on résumés, job titles, and past history. Most meaningful capability signals are unstructured and therefore lost, fragmented, or never captured at all.

## II. From systems of record to systems of intelligence

Maki frames AI not as a new interface, but as a new execution layer. In this view:

- older HR technology stored shallow information
- AI agents actively convert hiring data into structured skill signals
- hiring systems move from passive storage to active intelligence

This is the foundation of Maki’s positioning: an intelligence engine for scientific hiring at scale.

Maki presents agents like:

- `Mochi` for conversational engagement, interviewing, and assessment
- `Tomo` for turning interviews into structured evidence and clearer decisions

The shift is from software that helps teams manage work to intelligence that can execute and scale core parts of the hiring process.

## III. Talent becomes a continuous system

Maki argues that hiring, development, mobility, and growth are not separate processes but parts of the same loop. The common language across those stages is skills.

According to this view:

- the same intelligence that assesses candidates can help understand employees
- skills can reveal potential, identify gaps, and support mobility
- organizations can move from fragmented HR systems to a unified system of human understanding

## IV. Compound intelligence becomes the new moat

Maki distinguishes between automation and compound intelligence:

- automation makes work faster
- compound intelligence makes each cycle of work improve the system

In a compound system:

- every conversation creates structured signal
- every decision creates measurable data
- every outcome becomes feedback
- every cycle improves future precision

Over time, intelligence becomes an asset rather than just a feature: a self-reinforcing model of what predicts success, performance, growth, and retention.

## V. The tipping point

Maki argues that this transition is already underway in large organizations across industries. The result is presented not as incremental efficiency, but as structural transformation:

- recruiter productivity improves
- hiring decisions become more consistent and measurable
- candidate experience scales without losing depth

The company’s broader claim is that organizations adopting compound intelligence will evolve faster than those staying with linear, manual processes.

## VI. Trust is the condition for scale

Maki explicitly states that this transformation only matters if it is safe. Because hiring affects people’s lives, AI in talent systems must be:

- auditable
- governed
- transparent
- privacy-first
- designed to reduce bias rather than amplify it

Trust is positioned not as a secondary requirement, but as the precondition for adoption and scale.

## VII. The human future

Maki’s final claim is that it is building an intelligence layer for human potential, not to replace humans, but to amplify them.

For organizations, this means:

- more measurable hiring
- more consistent decisions
- continuous improvement in talent decisions

For individuals, it means:

- fairer processes
- better matching
- less randomness
- more opportunity

## Core thesis

The central thesis of the manifesto is that the future of work will be shaped not only by intelligence, but by how intelligence compounds around people over time.
