# Maki AI Use And Candidate Rights

## Purpose

This document summarizes how Maki explains AI use to candidates and how recruiters are expected to respond when candidates ask about AI or decisions.

## Candidate-facing explanation of AI use

Maki states that AI may be involved at several points in an assessment journey, depending on the assessment type.

### 1. Conversational interviewing

If a candidate completes a voice-based conversational screening, the interaction may be run by an AI agent called `Mochi`.

Mochi’s described role:

- ask questions
- listen to answers
- guide the conversation
- stay within the framework defined by the recruiter

Maki explicitly states that Mochi does not make hiring decisions.

### 2. Transcription and information retrieval

If a candidate answers verbally through audio or video, speech-recognition tools may convert those answers into text. The resulting text can then be used for evaluation.

### 3. Score calculation

Maki explains that AI may analyze responses and calculate results for some assessment types using predefined scoring grids.

Its explanation emphasizes that:

- scoring depends on the assessment type
- results are based on answers and predefined evaluation grids
- AI does not use personal information such as name, age, or gender to produce results

### 4. Proctoring and irregularity detection

Maki explains that automated proctoring may be used to detect signals of possible irregularities.

The stated position is:

- proctoring outputs are only indicators
- they do not automatically disqualify a candidate
- a human reviews them

### 5. Candidate guidance and feedback

Maki says AI may be used to:

- generate personalized scorecards
- create feedback or improvement suggestions
- power an "Ask me anything" chatbot during the assessment

### 6. Recruiter guidance

Maki also states that AI may support recruiters by:

- suggesting interview plans
- recommending relevant questions
- helping structure interviews

This support is described as separate from final decision-making.

## What Maki says AI does not do

Maki repeatedly states that AI does not decide:

- whether a candidate is selected
- whether a candidate is rejected
- whether a candidate is invited to the next stage

Recruiters remain the decision-makers, using Maki outputs only as decision-support signals.

## Candidate rights referenced

Maki’s candidate-facing materials reference `Article 86` of the `EU AI Act` and state that candidates have a right to obtain clear explanations about:

- the role of AI in the assessment process
- whether recruiters used outputs from AI features in a decision
- how those outputs contributed, where relevant

Candidates are directed to:

- the company that invited them to the assessment
- or Maki directly via `candidates@makipeople.com`

## Recruiter guidance for responding to candidate questions

Maki provides recruiter-facing guidance for answering questions such as:

- how was my score calculated?
- did AI reject me?
- what role did AI play?
- why was I not selected?

## Main recruiter guidance points

Recruiters are told to:

- explain that final decisions are made by humans
- explain AI’s role in processing, transcription, scoring, or interview support
- avoid saying that a score automatically determined the outcome
- review detailed results before deciding
- avoid rejecting candidates solely on automated scores
- keep records of AI-related candidate requests and responses

## Information recruiters do not have to disclose

Maki explicitly says recruiters do not need to reveal:

- internal scoring thresholds
- internal benchmarks
- proprietary selection criteria

The requirement is framed instead as giving a clear and human explanation of:

- the role of AI
- the fact that a human made the final decision

## Candidate scorecard sharing guidance

Maki instructs recruiters that if a candidate requests detailed results, they can share the candidate assessment report by:

- opening the candidate page in Maki
- clicking the three dots at the top right of the overall score
- selecting `Copy link to assessment report`

It warns recruiters not to share the hiring-manager scorecard with candidates.

## Contact points

- Candidate support: `candidates@makipeople.com`
- Recruiter support: `recruiters@makipeople.com`

## Core practical takeaway

Maki’s AI-rights messaging is built around a consistent line:

- AI may conduct, transcribe, structure, and score parts of the process
- AI may generate support signals or feedback
- the hiring decision itself remains human-led
